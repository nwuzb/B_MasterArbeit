\subsection{Overview}

In this study, we carefully selected four datasets, combining large-scale public datasets with an in-house dataset, to develop more efficient and accurate methods for crowd head detection, tracking, and counting on railway platforms.

\subsection{Dataset for the YOLO detector}

\textbf{CrowdHuman} \cite{shao2018crowdhuman} is a large-scale, richly annotated, and diverse benchmark dataset that covers a wide range of scenes, illumination conditions, densities, ages, and skin tones. Each human instance is annotated with a head bounding box, a visible-region bounding box, and a full-body bounding box, with a particular focus on addressing occlusion in crowded scenes. The dataset contains 15,000 training images, 4,370 validation images, and 5,000 test images. Across the training and validation subsets there are approximately 470K human instances, averaging about 22--23 persons per image, spanning from negative samples to very high-density scenes with various occlusion patterns. We focus on head detection, where head sizes vary widely across images. We use the 15,000 training images and the 4,370 validation images as the training and testing sets, respectively, for the first-stage training of our YOLOv11msize model.

\subsection{Fine-tuning datasets for the YOLO detector}
For fine-tuning, we curate and annotate our own dataset by integrating three different sources. The annotated data are used to fine-tune the detector trained above.

\paragraph{Open Sensor Data for Rail 2023 \cite{OpenSensorDataforRail2023}} This dataset comprises 45 annotated multi-sensor sequences (RGB camera, infrared camera, LiDAR, radar, localization, IMU). The image resolution is 2464\,\(\times\)\,1600, and the data were collected on various railway tracks in Hamburg, Germany. We use the RGB sequences captured from the middle camera at the train front, extracting one frame every 50 frames to reduce redundancy, resulting in 20 images and 180 human instances in total. We annotate head bounding boxes using Roboflow and include these images in the second-stage fine-tuning.

\paragraph{RailEye3D Dataset \cite{RailEye3D_Dataset}} This dataset targets train platform scenes for passenger safety and train scheduling applications. The camera is mounted on the side of the train. It consists of 10 image sequences recorded at six stations in Austria, with a resolution of 1920\,\(\times\)\,1088. We obtained the images via email request and sample one frame every 60 frames, yielding 320 images and 1,221 human instances. We annotate head bounding boxes using Roboflow and include these images in the second-stage fine-tuning.

\end{figure}

\paragraph{TrainPlatformCrowd Dataset (ours)} We construct an image dataset and annotate human heads using rectangular boxes in YOLO format. The images are extracted from publicly available YouTube videos covering 60 different platform scenes and crowd densities. The camera viewpoint corresponds to a front-mounted camera positioned near the center above the train nose. To reduce redundancy, we extract at least one frame every 30 frames, resulting in 1,660 images and 5,831 human head instances. Image resolutions vary across the collected material.

Overall, we assemble 2,000 head-only images for fine-tuning, split 1,400/600 into training and validation sets, respectively. We set \texttt{backbone=10} and fine-tune the detector to obtain the \texttt{YOLO11freeze10.pt} weights, which are used as the detector in the tracking system.

\subsection{MOT dataset: MOT-TrainPlatformCrowdHead (MOT-TPCH)}

To thoroughly validate the performance of our proposed system, especially in railway platform scenarios of interest, we construct an MOT dataset by annotating continuous head trajectories in MOT format across 27 video clips. The clips are sourced from publicly available YouTube videos and our own recordings. The camera is mounted above the train nose, facing forward at a fixed position (see schematic Figure P8), and the footage typically covers the process of the train entering the station and decelerating to a stop. Our targets are located on the platform. Clip length and frame rate vary; the capture device and viewpoint also differ slightly across clips. Within a sequence, the number of targets ranges from 3 to 124, covering diverse illumination, occlusion, and crowd density conditions. We use the MOT-format videos to train the ReID MobileNet-BO model and to evaluate system performance.

\paragraph{Pre-processing} We first select and crop temporal segments from the downloaded videos, prioritizing the highest-available resolution (2560\,\(\times\)\,1440 or 3840\,\(\times\)\,2160). Temporal cropping begins roughly when the nearest target is about 30 meters away and ends when the last target leaves the frame or the train comes to a stop. Spatially, we crop to focus on the platform side while reducing both width and height to 60\% of the original to balance quality and file size. When the platform is on the right, we crop a rectangle anchored at the bottom-right corner with height 0.6\,H and width 0.6\,W; when the platform is on the left, we similarly crop from the bottom-left corner. During processing, we remove audio. Consequently, 3840\,\(\times\)\,2160 videos become 2304\,\(\times\)\,1296, and 2560\,\(\times\)\,1440 videos become 1536\,\(\times\)\,864, whereas 1920\,\(\times\)\,1080 videos retain their resolution and are only temporally trimmed. The core \texttt{ffmpeg} invocation is shown below:

\begin{minted}[fontsize=\small, linenos, breaklines]{python}
# Construct ffmpeg command
crop_filter = f"crop={crop_w}:{crop_h}:{crop_x}:{crop_y}"
cmd = [
    FFMPEG_PATH, "-y",
    "-ss", start_time,
    "-to", end_time,
    "-i", video_path,
    "-vf", crop_filter,
    "-c:v", "libx264",
    "-crf", "15",  # High quality, almost lossless
    "-preset", "veryslow",
    "-an",  # Remove audio
    out_path
]
\end{minted}

\paragraph{Summary} In total, we use 21,950 images and approximately 500K object instances across all experiments. Of these, we ourselves annotated 2,000 images and 27 video sequences (24,788 frames, 89,087 bounding boxes, 885 object identities), amounting to about 23K object instances. This diverse data strategy ensures that our models perform strongly not only in generic crowded scenes but also in the high-density, dynamic environment of railway platforms, enabling accurate counting and laying a solid foundation for further optimization and application.

三、MOT数据集MOT-TrainPlatformCrowdHead Dataset（MOT-TPCH）

为了充分地验证我们的所以提出的系统的性能，特别是在我们关注的火车站台的场景下，我们采取了自建MOT数据集的方式。通过采用MOT格式对27个视频片段的人类头部进行连续轨迹标注，得到了我们的数据集 MOT-TrainPlatformCrowdHead Dataset。这些视频片段主要来自Youtube公开视频中的截取的视频片段，以及我们自己拍摄的类似场景视频，该视频系列的主要特点是拍摄设备位于火车头的上面，正视前进的方向固定位置拍摄，如图P8,展示了摄像头和我们关注的场景的位置关系。视频展示了火车进站、减速到停止的过程，我们关注的目标位于站台上，每个视频的长度，帧率不尽相同，拍摄的设备未知，以及拍摄的位置略有不同。在一段视频序列当中，我们的目标数量从3到124不等，涵盖不同的光照、遮挡、人群密度条件，时长从几秒到几十秒不等。我们将所具有MOT标注格式的视频用于训练ReID MobileNet-BO模型以及用于评估系统性能。

处理的方式
首先，我们将Youtube下载好的视频进行片段的选择和裁剪，我们的原则是，尽可能地下载该视频的高分辨率版本，我们得到了2560x1440或3840x2160分辨率的多个视频，我们在时序上裁剪的原则的是从距离第一个目标约30m左右作为被裁剪片段的开始，从最后一个目标刚从画面消失或者列车刚好停止结束；画面尺寸上的处理则是以人群所在的一侧为主要画面进行裁剪，考虑我们来处理数据的设备的局限，我们将画面按照原尺寸的长和宽的0.6倍进行裁剪，具体是，当火车进站的时候，如果人群在右边站台，那么我们将拍摄的画面以右下角为原点，向上对高裁剪0.6倍高度，向左对宽裁剪0.6倍的宽度，我们保留这部分尺寸的连续画面我们最终的视频，如图P7；如果人群在左边站台，那么我们将拍摄的画面以左下角为原点，向上对高裁剪0.6倍高度，向右对宽裁剪0.6倍的宽度，我们保留这部分这部分尺寸的连续画面作为我们最终的视频，与此同时为了减小视频的体积，我们在裁剪的过程中删除了视频的音频。这意味着，原3840x2160的视频经过处理后得到了分辨率为2304x1296像素的视频，原2560x1440经过处理后得到的是1536x864像素分辨率的视频，然后我们保留了1920x1080的视频尺寸，只是在时序上进行了裁剪得到我们关注的视频片段。我们在尽可能保证画面原始质量和文件大小平衡的情况下进行，使用ffmpeg技术裁剪的核心代码如下所示：
    \begin{minted}[fontsize=\small, linenos, breaklines]{python}
    # Construct ffmpeg command
    crop_filter = f"crop={crop_w}:{crop_h}:{crop_x}:{crop_y}"
    cmd = [
        FFMPEG_PATH, "-y",
        "-ss", start_time,
        "-to", end_time,
        "-i", video_path,
        "-vf", crop_filter,
        "-c:v", "libx264",
        "-crf", "15",  # High quality, almost lossless
        "-preset", "veryslow",
        "-an",  # Remove audio
        out_path
    ]
    \end{minted}
